Every Tiranga and Sacred stick starts in a pair of hands. Across our units, more than a thousand women roll the masala onto bamboo, lay the sticks out to dry, check them and pack them into the cartons that reach shops in 500+ cities.

The photograph above was taken on the steps of one of our plants. Blue hairnets and white gloves are part of the working day here, so that what goes into the paste stays clean from the first roll to the sealed box.

## Why the hands matter

A machine can push paste onto a stick. It cannot feel when the masala is too wet, notice a stick that will burn unevenly, or set aside a batch that does not smell right. That judgement is what our artisans bring, and it is why "hand-rolled" is the first of our standards rather than a line on the pack.

## Stories, by name

The Sacred Thought is where we will introduce some of these women one at a time: how they came to the work, what they are proud of, and what they would like the people lighting our agarbatti to know. We are starting with the people on our floors.
